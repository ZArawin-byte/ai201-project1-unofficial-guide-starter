How to Pick Professors at Lakemont — Advising Wiki (unofficial)

Rule one: the official course catalog tells you nothing about what a class is actually like. Two sections of the same course number can be completely different experiences depending on who teaches it. Always check the section instructor before you register, not just the course.

Rule two: ratings sites are useful but read the WHY, not the star number. A professor rated 2.5 stars who's marked "tough but you'll learn a lot" is very different from a 2.5 rated "disorganized and never responds to email." Sort reviews by recent — a professor who was great five years ago may have changed, or vice versa.

Rule three: ask about exam style specifically. At Lakemont the biggest workload differences come down to whether exams are cumulative and whether they're based on lecture slides or the textbook. Slide-based exams reward attendance; textbook-based reward reading. Neither is easier — they just demand different habits.

Rule four: "weed-out" intro courses (Intro Chem, Calc II, Intro Econ) are graded on a strict curve. Your grade depends on the class average, so forming a study group actually helps everyone, not just you. The students who isolate during weed-out courses tend to struggle most.

Rule five: office hours are wildly underused. Most professors sit in empty offices during posted hours. Going even once means they know your name, which matters for recommendation letters later. The students who get the best letters are the ones who showed up, not necessarily the ones with the highest grades.
